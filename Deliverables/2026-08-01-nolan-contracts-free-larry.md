# NOLAN — free Larry by taking work OFF his attention queue, not by adding a person

**Blunt answer to your question first.** You do **not** need a new hire, and Silas must **not** absorb Larry's duties — that is precisely the crossover you want to avoid, and it would dilute the one thing Silas is good for (his narrow schema/data lane). The duties Larry drops under load — the Tower/Codex pre-merge call and the devbot "finished" pings — are dropped **because they compete with execution inside Larry's single attention queue.** You cannot fix a dropped notification by *reminding* an overloaded agent to send it; you fix it by making it fire from an **event, not from his memory.** The "missing role" you feel is the **external verifier — a different MODEL (Codex/Tower), not a persona.** Hiring a same-model persona to verify Larry is self-certification with extra steps: no independence gained, admin added.

Your thesis is correct and it is the load-bearing insight: **the less Larry does himself, the less he forgets what you asked.** So the design rule is — *everything deterministic leaves his queue entirely; everything skilled but not-orchestration routes to a specialist; the irreversible endpoints move behind an external gate he cannot skip.* What remains is pure orchestration. That directly serves your Rule 4 (**enforce via capability boundaries, not prose**): Larry does not "promise to remember" the ping — he no longer holds the job at all.

## B. Is a new hire warranted? No — the "missing role" is the external verifier, and it is a MODEL, not a persona

Correcting explicitly for HR's bias to answer "yes, hire someone": **hiring adds a same-model context that shares Larry's exact failure signature (over-build, self-certify, forget) and adds standing admin — the disease you are trying to cure.** The test for a real hire is: *is there a recurring, SKILLED (judgement-requiring, non-deterministic) duty, outside Silas's and Keel's lanes, that Larry drops and that no mechanism can carry?* Walking the list, there isn't one:

- The dropped pings (#6) and delivery tracking (#4) are **deterministic and event-triggered** — you do not hire a person to remember to send a notification; you wire the event. A hire here would just be a second thing to forget.
- The dropped pre-merge review (#7) is a **verification** duty, and verification's whole value is **independence**. The only independent actor on this estate is a **different model** (Codex/Tower caught the HIGH bug Larry missed; the 1,440 lines of gates caught nothing). A hired persona runs on the same model — it would *certify Larry with Larry*, which is exactly the root defect from the RCA. So the "missing role" resolves to **an external different-model gate you already have**, wired to *block* the merge rather than wait to be invoked.

**On Silas absorbing duties:** do not. His contract earns its keep precisely because it is narrow (schema, durable state, integrity *decisions*). Pour orchestration admin into it and you get a diluted Silas *and* a new crossover with Larry — two contracts made worse to save zero hires. The boundary you asked to protect is protected by **not** doing this.

**The one honest caveat.** If, after automation, you still want a *human-named* owner for the Warwick-facing relay (pings + delivery comms) so there is a throat to choke when it breaks — that owner is **Mack**, not a new hire: Mack already owns the *operation of released services*, and a notifier daemon is a released service. That is a one-line boundary extension to an existing contract (Mack operates the relay he does not author), **not** a new role, and it still keeps the *performance* automated so nothing depends on anyone's memory.

## C. Acceptance bar: does every substantive recurring task end with an owner other than Larry?

Yes — with git named honestly.

- Duties #1–5, #9–12: **owner = a deterministic mechanism** (automation), maintained by Mack where a human owner is wanted. Off Larry's queue.
- Duty #6 (pings): **owner = event-triggered notifier** (Mack-maintained). Off Larry's queue — the fix to your exact evidence.
- Duty #7 (pre-merge verification): **owner = external different-model gate** (Codex/Tower). Off Larry's queue.
- Duties #13–15 (read-back, routing, integration): **these are not tasks to offload — they are the orchestrator role itself.** The acceptance bar is about *substantive recurring work*; orchestration is what remains *after* the work is offloaded, by design.
- Duty #8 (git): the **only** item that cannot fully leave Larry without crossover — Keel is forbidden to merge/push, Silas would be crossover — **and it should not leave him**, because integration is an orchestration act. Its mechanics are automated and its irreversible endpoints are externally gated, so what stays with Larry is a *decision*, not *labour*. **No new contract is warranted here**; a "release-engineer" hire to own merges would collide with both Keel and your own git-ownership rule.

**Minimal new contract, if you overrule me:** none is needed. The smallest thing that could look like a "new role" is the Warwick-facing relay, and its correct home is a boundary line added to **Mack**, scoped to *operate* (not author) the notifier — one role touched, zero roles created.

## D. Does the named-specialist team earn its keep every session?

**No — not on the every-session path.** Contracts are valuable as **durable, dispatch-time artifacts**: when Larry routes a piece of work, the specialist's contract is exactly the right thing to load. What does not earn its keep is the **standing tax** — reading ~18 contracts, the build-team table, the expansion scan, and the roster ceremony on *every* boot, most of it for specialists never dispatched. **Load contracts on dispatch; keep them off the boot path.** Keep active, boot-visible contracts to the handful actually run (the domain services, Keel's implementation pattern, and the different-model verifier as a gate not a persona). Archive the dormant ones — retrievable when routed, invisible until then. That, plus the table above, is the whole reset: Larry's queue holds orchestration and nothing else, so the request you make is the thing he is actually paying attention to.
