- **Governance head:** cde4d870bf7b03af77f17db4251af471720e378b

## Executive summary

**Recommended pairing: engineering/disaster "what went wrong" explainers (the B-side, not the front-runner), produced documentary-style with a programmatic Remotion assembly pipeline, ElevenLabs narration, and stock footage + code-generated charts.** Marginal cash cost lands around **£5–10 per finished 8–10 min video** plus a mandatory human editorial pass. The money/finance niche remains viable as a second choice but carries materially higher saturation and a UK defamation exposure the disaster niche does not. The single most important external fact: **YouTube's July-2025 "inauthentic content" policy and its January-2026 enforcement wave target exactly the fully-automated end of this plan** — the pipeline's citation discipline and a human editorial pass per video are not nice-to-haves, they are the monetisation survival strategy. Second most important: **the YPP watch-hour bar doubles to 8,000 hours on 2027-02-01**, which a channel started now will almost certainly face.

**Time-to-monetisation.** Current YPP (confirmed against [Google's own help pages](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/72851) and [vidiq](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/youtube-partner-program-guide/), 2026-09-03): fan-funding tier at 500 subs + 3,000 watch hours; **ad-revenue tier at 1,000 subs + 4,000 public watch hours (12 mo) or 10M Shorts views (90 days)**. **Change confirmed via [TechCrunch, 2026-08-10](https://techcrunch.com/2026/08/10/youtube-now-requires-creators-to-have-twice-as-many-watch-hours-to-start-earning-money/): from 2027-02-01 the long-form bar doubles to 8,000 qualified watch hours (Shorts: 20M views/90 days); existing YPP members are unaffected.** Confidence: High. Planning implication: a channel launched Q4 2026 will not plausibly clear 4,000 hours before February and should therefore plan against the 8,000-hour bar — realistically **9–18 months of consistent weekly publishing before first ad revenue**, and the blog-claimed "3–6 months" ramps (Low confidence) predate the doubling.

## 2. Platform enforcement — the inauthentic content policy

**What the policy actually says** (primary source: [YouTube channel monetization policies](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392), read 2026-09-03). Renamed from "repetitious content" on 2025-07-15. Monetised content must not be "mass-produced, generic, repetitive, or manipulative." Explicitly disallowed, quoted: *"AI-generated content made with generic or unoriginal templates giving the impression of mass production without adding the creator's original, authentic insights or perspective"*; *"image slideshows, templated storylines, or scrolling text with minimal or no narrative, commentary, or educational value."* Explicitly allowed: consistent formats where "the bulk of your content is different," and *"creative tools to assist in delivering a unique, well-researched, or creative narrative."* **A well-researched cited narrative is, verbatim, the safe harbour.** Confidence: High (primary).

**What enforcement has actually caught.** January 2026: **16 channels terminated or wiped, ~35M combined subscribers, 4.7B lifetime views, estimated $10M/yr ad revenue** ([TheNextWeb, 2026-06-15](https://thenextweb.com/news/youtube-ai-slop-crackdown-faceless-creators-collateral-damage); echoed by [TechTimes](https://www.techtimes.com/articles/320629/20260715/youtube-wiped-35m-subscribers-over-ai-slop-now-its-judging-your-taste.htm) and multiple creator-economy blogs). The $10M figure appears to trace to one origin — treat the revenue number as **single-source**. Scale context: a Kapwing study (Oct 2025, via [Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/youtubes-ai-slop-problem-and-how-marketers-can-compete/567297/)) found 278 pure-slop channels among 15,000 trending channels with 63B views and est. $117M/yr — single-origin study, flagged as such. Quiet video- and channel-level demonetisations of high-volume AI channels through late 2025 are widely reported but individually unverifiable.

**Collateral damage — the part that matters for us.** Per TheNextWeb (Medium confidence, one journalistic source plus creator testimony): enforcement is **channel-level**, algorithmic proxies **cannot distinguish a one-person faceless channel from a bot farm**, and legitimate human faceless creators report demonetisation ("the people who do the same content as me without their face in it, most of them are getting demonetised" — Doctor NOS, 1.7M subs). Niche *educational* faceless content is reported as more resilient than broad-topic.

**What survivors do** (cross-blog consensus + policy text, Medium confidence): disclose synthetic elements via the YouTube Studio "altered content" checkbox (mandatory since late 2023 for realistic synthetic media; non-disclosure escalates warning → 90-day monetisation suspension → YPP removal); keep a human editorial pass on every video; vary structure video-to-video; add original analysis/perspective; use high-quality narration (robotic TTS is a named reviewer red flag); show sources on screen. **Our citation discipline is precisely an "originality signal" as YouTube defines it.**

## 3. Production stacks

### (a) AI animal-vlog genre (Veo-style stitched 8-second clips)

- **Toolchain:** Google Veo 3/3.1 via Gemini API (or Flow / AI Ultra subscription ~$249/mo), prompt-per-scene, 8s clips with native audio, stitched in any editor. Guides: [veo3ai.io](https://www.veo3ai.io/blog/how-to-use-veo-3-for-youtube-2026), [Filmora capybara guide](https://filmora.wondershare.com/trending-topic/ai-capybara-video.html).
- **Cost per finished minute** (computed from [Gemini API pricing](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/pricing) tiers as reported: Standard $0.40/s with audio, Fast ~$0.10–0.15/s, Lite $0.05/s — Medium confidence, aggregator-reported against a primary page): Standard = **$24/min raw**; at a realistic 2–3× generate-to-keep ratio, **$50–70 (£40–55) per finished minute**. Fast tier ≈ $9/min raw, ~£15–20/min finished. An 8-minute video at Standard quality: **£300–450**. This genre lives on Shorts economics for a reason.
- **Monetisation status:** this genre is the *bullseye* of the inauthentic-content enforcement described in §2. Individual channel fates (including the meerkat-type channels) are **UNVERIFIED** — no reliable public record of which specific animal-vlog channels are monetised vs demonetised. The structural fact stands: templated generated clips at scale is the named disallowed category.

### (b) AI cartoon/animated story channels

- **Toolchain (all single-source, vendor-adjacent — Low confidence on specifics):** end-to-end story platforms ([LongStories.ai](https://www.neolemon.com/blog/ai-cartoon-generators-content-creators-youtube-tiktok/) for character-consistent long-form; Higgsfield for Pixar-style), or Veo/Flow + ChatGPT pipelines. The hard technical problem is character consistency across scenes; the platforms sell exactly that.
- **Degree of automation:** high — script-in, video-out. Which is exactly the policy problem: the more turnkey, the more "templated storyline." No cost advantage over (c/d) for our subject matter, and our evidence-pack differentiator adds nothing to cartoons. Not recommended; not costed further.

### (c) Documentary/explainer (stock footage + TTS + motion graphics + charts)

- **Standard toolchain:** LLM script → TTS narration (§4) → stock B-roll ([Storyblocks](https://www.storyblocks.com) ~$21–30/mo annual for unlimited downloads; [Envato Elements](https://elements.envato.com) ~$16.50/mo; Pexels/Pixabay free tiers) → charts/motion graphics (After Effects templates, or code — see (d)) → assembly in DaVinci Resolve (free) / CapCut / Premiere. Licence note: Storyblocks and Envato subscription licences cover monetised YouTube use (vendor-stated; Medium confidence — verify the exact licence tier before first upload).
- **What automates:** script (ours, already built), narration, B-roll *search* (APIs exist), chart generation, subtitle/thumbnail generation. **What doesn't:** B-roll *selection* against the narrative, pacing, and the editorial QC pass — that residual human layer is also the monetisation safe harbour, so automating it away is strategically wrong, not just hard.

### (d) Programmatic assembly — Remotion, FFmpeg, JSON2Video

- **[Remotion](https://www.remotion.dev/docs/license/pricing)** (React/Node, video-as-code): **free for individuals and for-profit teams ≤3 people, commercial use explicitly included** — confirmed against the vendor licence page and [GitHub LICENSE.md](https://github.com/remotion-dev/remotion/blob/main/LICENSE.md), 2026-09-03 (High confidence, primary). Company licence only at 4+ people ($25/seat/mo or $0.01/render). Mature, production-used, renders locally at £0 marginal cost. Natural fit: our pipeline already emits structured JSON (script + citations + assets) — Remotion consumes exactly that shape and can render on-screen source citations, charts and kinetic type deterministically.
- **FFmpeg:** free (LGPL/GPL), commercial use fine, the assembly substrate under everything.
- **[JSON2Video](https://json2video.com/pricing/):** hosted API alternative, from $19.95/mo, watermarked free tier, mature per [Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/p/275517/JSON2Video/) listings (Medium confidence). Siblings Shotstack/Creatomate exist — **not researched, UNVERIFIED**.
- **Verdict on maturity:** Remotion + FFmpeg locally is the lowest-cost, highest-control route and the licence position is unambiguous. Hosted APIs buy convenience, not capability.

## 4. TTS for long-form narration

- **Quality benchmark:** ElevenLabs (v3) remains the consensus quality leader for expressive narration in 2026 ([eesel comparison](https://www.eesel.ai/blog/elevenlabs-alternatives), [inworld](https://inworld.ai/resources/elevenlabs-alternatives) — secondary but consistent).
- **Cost:** ElevenLabs tiers $5 / $22 / $99 / $299/mo; effective **~$0.17–0.22 per audio minute** on Creator/Pro tiers (~1,000 chars/min heuristic; [vendor pricing](https://elevenlabs.io/pricing) via multiple 2026 aggregators — Medium confidence). A 10-min narration ≈ 9,000–10,000 chars ≈ **£1.50–2.00**, comfortably inside the $22/mo Creator tier (~100 min/mo) at weekly cadence.
- **Cheaper alternatives:** Fish Audio API ~$15/M chars (~80% cheaper, quality a step down); OpenAI TTS ~$15/M chars; Murf, Cartesia, Speechify in between (all secondary-sourced, Medium confidence).
- **Monetisation effect:** AI narration is **permitted**; the method of voice production is not the test — value and variation are ([support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392) + cross-blog consensus). **Low-quality robotic TTS is a named reviewer red flag**; premium narration is part of the compliance posture, not a luxury. Disclose synthetic narration via the altered-content checkbox.

## 6. Verdict

**Recommendation: Niche 2 (engineering/disaster "what went wrong") × style (c) rendered through (d) — a Remotion-based programmatic documentary pipeline with premium TTS, stock B-roll, code-generated diagrams, on-screen citations, and a mandatory human editorial pass per video.**

Why the B-side beats the front-runner: (i) the evidence-pack pipeline's citation discipline maps perfectly onto free, public-domain official accident reports — a moat no finance channel has; (ii) moderate saturation vs finance's worst-in-class; (iii) evergreen subject matter suits evenings-only cadence where AI news cannot; (iv) it avoids naming living scammers, which finance-scam content cannot. Money/finance remains the fallback if disaster content underperforms — the same pipeline serves both, so the switch cost is a topic change, not a rebuild.

**Top 3 risks, plainly:**

1. **Policy/enforcement risk — and it compounds with the automation ambition.** Enforcement is channel-level, algorithmically applied, and demonstrably hits legitimate faceless channels as collateral. The more Warwick automates, the closer the output drifts to the named disallowed category. The human editorial pass and per-video structural variation are load-bearing and permanently cap "largely automated" at roughly "automated except judgement."
2. **The monetisation ramp just doubled and the channel will face the new bar.** 8,000 watch hours from 2027-02-01 means realistically 9–18 months of consistent publishing before first ad revenue, in a niche where discovery — not production — is the bottleneck. This is a patience test, and every "3 months to monetisation" claim in the niche blogs predates the change.
3. **UK defamation exposure (applies mainly to the finance fallback).** "How X lost £4bn" and scam breakdowns name real people and companies under claimant-friendly English libel law. The citation verifier helps but does not immunise; the disaster niche, built on official investigation reports, largely designs this risk out.
