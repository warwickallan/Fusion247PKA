## Executive orientation

This is a first-person build log by YouTuber Jack Craig, who starts a brand-new, AI-generated YouTube Shorts channel from zero and documents it over 48 hours, explicitly so viewers can replicate the method. It combines a reverse-engineered content formula (borrowed from an established viral AI food channel), a concrete AI production pipeline (ChatGPT + Higgsfield + Premiere Pro), and a running commentary on YouTube's algorithm and monetization policy for AI content. ### 3. The reverse-engineered content formula ("phase structure")

From this analysis Jack derives a six-phase narrative structure he claims is the "general formula" behind the inspiration channel's success, illustrated with a 35M-view example video about "pinching 909,099 broccoli florets" [03:09]:

1. **Declare** — an opening declaration of what will be done (the stated absurd goal).
2. **Assessment** — inspection/measurement (e.g., florets measured).
3. **Isolate** — a repetitive, granular unit action performed one item at a time (e.g., salt inserted into each floret individually).
4. **Process** — an extended, often time-lapsed, intricate procedure.
5. **Build** — rapid assembly of all parts.
6. **Reveal** — the finished product shown.

He represents this as a graph/shape and calls it reusable across topics [03:37].

### 4. The three-criteria filter for choosing a new topic

Jack argues the formula only works if the new topic satisfies three criteria the inspiration channel also satisfies [03:37–04:59]:

1. **Universal relatability** — no niche knowledge needed; the inspiration channel used food, which everyone understands.
2. **Emotional hook** — specifically the emotion of *absurdity*: viewers perceive an irrational amount of effort spent on a pointless outcome and can't look away (evidenced by comments calling the original creator "insane").
3. **Completion compulsion** — the opening declaration creates an open loop; viewers stay to see it resolved, driving retention.

This is presented as a direct correction of what Jack calls misinformation/fear-mongering common among creators [25:34]. **Stated assumption**: a widespread belief (per Jack, often spread by creators "for views") that AI-generated content is broadly unmonetizable on YouTube. **Stated reversal**: YouTube's actual "inauthentic content" policy targets AI content specifically where it has (1) minimal variation across uploads, or (2) is easily replicable at scale — not AI content in general. ## Key concepts & takeaways

- **Study, don't replicate**: dissect a proven format's structure rather than copying its content, since YouTube suppresses direct duplicates.
- **Six-phase content arc**: Declare → Assessment → Isolate → Process → Build → Reveal, as a reusable narrative skeleton for this content genre.
- **Three-criteria topic filter**: universal relatability, emotional hook (absurdity), completion compulsion.
- **AI-enabled vs. AI slop**: the proposed dividing line for monetization eligibility is manual creative direction and non-trivial variation, not the mere presence of AI generation.
- **Test-push mechanics**: new videos get graduated algorithmic test pushes; average % viewed is the leading signal that determines whether pushes escalate.
- **Single-focus upload pacing**: don't split algorithmic attention across simultaneous uploads on a young channel.
- **Effort allocation matches monetization drivers**: skip cosmetic setup (banner) that doesn't affect views; prioritize thumbnail/title/description patterns that plausibly do.
