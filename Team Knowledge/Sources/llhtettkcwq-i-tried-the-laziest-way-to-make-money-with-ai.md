## Executive orientation

This is a first-person seven-day challenge video by Mark Tilbury (business/finance YouTuber) testing whether "AI influencers" — fully AI-generated personas built from Claude-generated images, video and voice — are a viable, low-effort way to make money, or just hype. He builds three AI influencers from scratch (health, wealth, relationship niches) using Claude + Higgsfield, posts content to Instagram, picks the best performer, builds and sells a digital product through it, and reports the real (modest) profit and cost breakdown at the end. The single reason it matters: it's an unusually complete, receipts-shown walkthrough of an end-to-end AI-influencer production pipeline (character generation → image → voice → video → distribution → monetisation) with real cost/revenue numbers, not just a hype claim.

## What the source says

### The premise and challenge rules
Tilbury frames this as testing viral claims that people are making thousands (one claim cited: $100,000 in 30 days) from AI influencers. He sets himself a self-imposed 7-day, sub-$99 budget challenge, explicitly barred from using his own face or voice (removing his usual on-camera advantage), with a personal $1,000 forfeit to a commenter if he fails to make at least $1,000 [00:18–01:07]. This is a stated personal-credibility/stunt framing as much as a tutorial — the wager and honesty-about-failure framing is itself part of the content's design (an audience-trust mechanism), not incidental.

### Competitive research findings (market reconnaissance)
Before building anything, he studies existing AI influencer accounts to reverse-engineer what works:
- **Lil Miquela** — established AI supermodel, 2M+ followers, reported ~$11M/year via brand deals with major brands (Prada, Calvin Klein) [01:17–01:57]. Evidence this niche can scale to full brand-partnership money, not just small affiliate income.
- **Mother Satori** — AI "108-year-old" holistic health expert; promotes a gut-cleanse/detox drink at the end of every video; large daily view counts and comments full of purchase requests [02:00–02:47]. Establishes health/wellness + a single recurring product promotion as a working pattern.
- Unnamed lifestyle/relationship-drama female persona, a fake "hardworking British woodworker" male persona, and an "AI granny" with 2M+ followers doing novelty content [02:55–03:39]. He explicitly flags an ethics concern here: the woodworker persona is deceptive because "the person behind the account is lying to their customers" — but notes it is "almost certainly" still profitable. This is a stated ethical caveat that is NOT dismissed, but also doesn't stop him proceeding — the video keeps disclosure-in-bio as its own practical compromise, discussed below.

From this research he concludes three niches dominate: **health, wealth, relationships**. He observes a gap: **no accounts he found covered "money/wealth" content itself**, which he frames as a market opportunity [03:39–04:03]. He also gives a generalisable rule: lean into personal interests/expertise for differentiation, and to hedge risk, build three influencers (one per niche) simultaneously rather than betting on one [04:03–04:28].

### Build pipeline (step by step, materially the core of the video)
1. **Character concepting** — uses Claude (free plan) with a simple prompt ("create a character profile for an AI influencer in the [niche] niche — name, backstory, distinct look, USP") to generate three personas: **Amos** (101-year-old traditional-remedies health guru), **Grandma Vivian** (self-made wealthy grandmother, deliberately positioned as the opposite of the "young man in a rented Lambo" wealth-influencer stereotype — an explicit counter-positioning strategy), and **Sienna** (22-year-old relationship/dating-drama persona) [04:28–06:13].
2. **Image generation** — signs up to **Higgsfield** (an AI image/video generation platform), using an email signup and a discount code ("Tilbury," capped at first 5,000 users) to get the "Ultra" plan (~100 videos) under budget [06:35–08:37]. Connects Higgsfield to Claude via Claude's **Connectors** feature (customize → connectors → add custom connector, paste link) so both tools can be driven from one chat [09:05–09:26]. Has Claude auto-write detailed image prompts (multiple angles, full body + closeup, physical description, clothing, white background, "realistic skin") rather than hand-writing them himself. Iterates on outputs (e.g., "make him a few years older" when the first-pass Amos looked too young) [09:26–10:32]. Generates supporting location/scene images per character matched to their persona (farmhouse porch/kitchen/garden for Amos; private jet/sports car/drawing room for Vivian; stylish locations for Sienna) [10:32–11:36].
3. **Script/content generation via Claude Skills** — introduces **Claude Skills** as "reusable tools with instructions already baked in" that give consistent output without rewriting long prompts each time. He uses a free, third-party-sourced "content engine skill" (installed via customize → add → upload skill) to generate content ideas per character, then generate full scripts from selected ideas [12:00–13:42]. Explicit caveat: he says he isn't sure how good these skills are and is trying them without vetting — an honesty flag about an unverified component of his own pipeline.
4. **Voice generation** — done directly on the Higgsfield website (audio tab): uploads character images, writes a short sample line, and has Claude generate a "detailed voice prompt" (persona-appropriate voice description) which is pasted into Higgsfield to generate the character's speaking voice [13:56–15:18].
5. **Video assembly** — uses Higgsfield's "Cinema Studio," creates a per-character project, sets 9:16 aspect ratio (vertical, short-form) and 1080p. First generates non-talking B-roll style clips (for adding on-screen text — a format he says performs well as a lower-risk test before generating talking-head content), referencing the character's earlier generated "character sheet" image to maintain visual consistency across outputs, then generates talking clips using another Claude Skill that expands a simple idea into a detailed video-generation prompt [15:29–17:32].
6. **Captioning and launch** — adds captions the next day using free tools (CapCut or Instagram Edits, explicitly noted as simple/accessible) [19:12–19:30]. Creates Instagram accounts for all three personas (handles, niche-relevant bio, profile picture chosen from generated images). Explicit disclosure choice: Amos's bio states "your AI grandpa" — Tilbury says this is deliberate so nobody feels "tricked," directly addressing the earlier-flagged ethics concern with a practical, partial mitigation (disclosure in bio, not necessarily in every video) [19:59–20:54]. Posts 10 videos per account and waits to compare performance, explicitly choosing not to declare a winner prematurely and to let data decide [21:08–21:29].

### The audience/social proof thread (a distinct, material vein)
While waiting, he checks comments and finds real audience engagement/arguments happening in Sienna's comments (people debating her choices, her outfit, where to buy her clothes) with essentially nobody in the comments noticing or objecting that she isn't a real person [21:37–22:20]. He flags this as personally surprising, given his own stated reservations about AI content ("AI slop") — this is a notable claim: audience deception/non-detection appears to be low-friction in practice, at least in his sample, and no one complained about it despite disclosure existing on the bios. This bears on the ethics thread earlier (deceptive personas are, per his own account, both effective and apparently tolerated).

### Results and the pivot/reversal (a major counterintuitive thread — preserve carefully)
Initial 10-post comparison: Vivian placed **third**, Sienna **second**, **Amos won** (with a clear gap over Sienna) [22:35–23:20]. Based on this, he commits fully to Amos and monetisation. **However**, by the final day, after doubling down on Amos, he reports that **Vivian's account "exploded"** organically — individual videos hitting 10,000, 20,000, and one hitting **250,000+ views**, going viral [29:07–29:35]. He explicitly states this is a reversal of his own decision: *"I should have trusted my gut and gone with Grandma Viv... imagine what we could have done."* This is the video's central counterintuitive/myth-reframing moment: **an early-performance signal (first 10 posts) picked the wrong long-term winner**, and he explicitly generalises the lesson — a short (7-day) challenge is good for testing viability but insufficient to see an account's "full picture" develop; more time is needed before committing [29:35–29:50]. This must not be folded into "Amos won" — the stated final takeaway contradicts the mid-video declared winner.

### Monetisation route selection and reasoning
With Amos as the (initially) chosen winner, he walks through the menu of AI-influencer monetisation methods identified during competitor research, and explicitly rules most out for this specific challenge (not universally):
- **Brand deals** — ruled out: too slow (outreach/negotiation) for a 7-day window [23:43–23:53].
- **Selling physical products / Shopify store** — ruled out as its own separate project; he floats it as a possible future video [23:53–24:01].
- **Instagram ad revenue** — ruled out as **not currently possible on Instagram Reels** (a stated platform-capability fact, not opinion) [24:05–24:14].
- **Affiliate income (e.g., TikTok Shop)** — identified as "probably one of the easiest and quickest" methods, but ruled out because they only launched on Instagram, not TikTok — explicitly flagged as **his own strategic mistake** ("in hindsight was probably a mistake") [24:14–24:21].
- **Digital products** — selected as the only method compatible with the constraints, and framed as a natural fit for an "educational" persona like Amos, produced entirely inside Claude [24:21–24:46].

### Digital product reasoning and build
He makes an explicit market-judgement claim: plain ebooks are no longer sellable because the same generic information is freely available via Google/ChatGPT; a sellable digital product now needs to be **structured, interactive, and transformation-oriented** (his term), not generic content [24:46–25:09]. This is presented as his own opinion/reasoning, not sourced externally. He designs a 30-day habit-building workbook for Amos (using another Claude Skill, "digital product skill") — Claude generates three product concepts with stated purchase-rationale for each, he selects "30-day challenge, one habit a day," and Claude builds the full workbook: daily named habits with checkboxes, a printable/trackable tracker page, and bonus content (recipes) [25:31–26:44]. He manually reviews the output for factual/safety soundness and to strip "AI slop" before selling — an explicit human-QA step on AI-generated advice content [27:15].

### Sales/distribution platform
Uses **Stan Store** (stated: not sponsored) for its 14-day free trial to host and sell the digital product, choosing a template, thumbnail, product name ("My 30-Day Energy Reset"), CTA button text, and pricing ($15.99 discounted to $12) [27:15–28:37]. Links the store from the Instagram bio. Notes bank details were connected off-camera for privacy (a minor but explicit privacy/operational note).

### Final financial reconciliation (concrete, load-bearing numbers)
- Revenue: 23 sales × $12 = **$276** [30:33].
- Costs: Claude (free tier — $0, but noted a mildly annoying wait for usage credits to refresh); Instagram Edits captions ($0); Stan Store 14-day trial ($0); PayPal payment-processing fees **$19.52** (explicitly excluded from the startup budget since it came off revenue, not upfront spend); Higgsfield full-price equivalent counted at **$99** (he explicitly refuses to count his own discount code toward the result "to keep it fair," even though he actually paid less) [30:33–31:46].
- **Total cost: $118.52. Net profit: $157.48** [31:46–32:13].
- He explicitly frames this as modest but proof-of-concept ("not life-changing... but let's put it into perspective") — achieved with minimal effort, a hard $99 budget, and only one of several possible monetisation paths tried on only one of three influencers [32:13–32:32].
- Since he set himself a $1,000 minimum-profit bar and missed it, he honours his own forfeit and will give $1,000 of his own money to a commenter, based on comments naming their favourite influencer [32:32–33:00].

### Explicitly stated future-scaling ideas (his own, not yet executed)
Cross-posting existing content to TikTok and YouTube Shorts at no extra production cost; trying all identified monetisation methods (not just digital products) across all three influencers, not just the winner; the physical-product/Shopify route as a separate future video [32:13–32:47]. These are stated intentions/ideas, not completed work.

### Ethics thread (recurring, not a one-off aside)
Tilbury raises the ethics question twice, unprompted: once when reviewing the deceptive "woodworker" competitor account (explicitly calling the underlying deception unethical while conceding it's still profitable) [03:17], and again at the end, stating he doesn't want to encourage over-adoption of AI personas because part of social media's value is genuine human connection, and fully AI-generated content "sucks the soul out of" that — framed explicitly as his personal opinion, "you can make your own mind up" [30:07–30:33]. This is presented alongside, not instead of, the practical viability conclusion — the video holds both "this works and is easy" and "I have reservations about it" simultaneously without resolving the tension.

## Mechanisms, methods & implementation detail

- **Claude + Higgsfield connector workflow**: Claude Customize → Connectors → Add → Add custom connector → paste Higgsfield connector link → Add. Once linked, image/video generation requests can be issued from within a single Claude chat rather than switching tools manually [09:05].
- **Claude Skills workflow**: Customize → Add → Upload a skill (drag-and-drop) to install; invoked in-chat via `/` command to select the skill (e.g., "content engine skill," "digital product skill," a video-prompt-expansion skill). Skills are described as reusable prompt/instruction bundles that produce consistent output without re-writing detailed prompts each time [12:00–12:22]. He sourced these third-party for free and states he did not vet their quality beforehand.
- **Character consistency technique**: subsequent image/video generations reference the earlier-generated "character sheet" image (explicitly described as "image one") so the persona's face/appearance stays consistent across outputs [16:39–17:13].
- **Higgsfield Cinema Studio settings**: new project per character; aspect ratio 9:16 (vertical/short-form); resolution 1080p [15:29–15:53].
- **Voice generation flow**: Higgsfield audio tab → upload character reference image(s) → provide a short sample script line → provide a "voice detail" prompt (generated via Claude) describing the desired voice → generate [14:20–15:00].
- **Product build flow**: `/` digital-product Claude Skill → request workbook for a named persona → Claude proposes multiple product concepts with purchase-rationale → select one → generate full structured workbook (daily content + tracker + bonus material) → attach a generated cover image → manual human review for accuracy/safety/AI-artifact removal before sale [25:31–27:15].
- **Sales setup flow (Stan Store)**: My Store → Add Products → Digital Products → choose template style → choose thumbnail image → set title/CTA button text/price → optional discount price → upload the actual digital file → Publish → copy resulting product link into Instagram bio; connect payment/bank details separately (done off-camera) [27:41–28:37].

## Key concepts & takeaways

- **AI influencer production pipeline**: persona concept (Claude) → image generation (Higgsfield, prompted by Claude) → voice generation (Higgsfield, voice-prompted by Claude) → video assembly (Higgsfield Cinema Studio) → captioning (free tools) → distribution (Instagram) → monetisation (digital product via Stan Store).
- **Claude Connectors** let an external generation tool (Higgsfield) be driven from within a single Claude conversation.
- **Claude Skills** are reusable instruction bundles that produce structured, consistent outputs (content ideas, scripts, prompts, products) without manual detailed prompting each time — installable from third parties, quality not guaranteed.
- **Character-sheet referencing** is the practical technique for maintaining visual consistency of an AI persona across many generated images/videos.
- **Counterintuitive reversal**: early performance data (first 10 posts) picked the "wrong" long-term winner; the account that lost the early comparison (Vivian) later went viral — a caution against short evaluation windows for judging viability.
- **Monetisation menu for AI influencers**: brand deals, physical product sales, ad revenue (platform-dependent — not available on IG Reels), affiliate income (platform-dependent — needs TikTok Shop), digital products (chosen here as fastest-fit for a short timeline and an "educational" persona).
- **Net result**: modest but real profit ($157.48) from a $99 budget within 7 days, using only one of several possible monetisation paths on only one of three built personas — presented as proof-of-concept rather than a mature business.
- **Unresolved ethical tension**: effectiveness and audience tolerance of undisclosed/semi-disclosed AI personas versus the creator's own discomfort with the practice — held open, not resolved, by the source itself.
